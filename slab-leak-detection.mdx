## Slab leak detection in Edmond, OK

Acoustic listening equipment and pressure testing are typically used to locate water leaks beneath a concrete building slab without demolishing the floor, an approach sometimes called foundation leak detection that aims to pinpoint the source of the leak precisely. Precision is the priority. Slab leak detection sits at the diagnostic end of the plumbing trade, finding the leak precisely before any concrete is cut or soil is excavated.

### Scope and component parts of slab leak detection

Slab leak detection covers the full diagnostic phase of a sub-slab plumbing failure, from initial pressure testing through final location marking on the slab surface. A technician begins by isolating the supply or drain line. A measured pressure differential in psi is then applied to confirm a leak is present before any listening equipment is deployed. Slab leak detection typically relies on four main methods: acoustic listening, thermal imaging, tracer gas analysis, and electronic amplification. Each method is suited to a different pipe material, slab depth, or leak size. Acoustic devices pick up the sound signature a pressurized leak produces through concrete. Thermal imaging reads the temperature anomaly that escaping water creates at the slab surface. Pressure sensors confirm the leak rate and help a technician rule out condensation or surface moisture before committing to a repair location.

Sub-slab leak detection is distinct from slab leak repair. Detection ends when the leak location is marked; repair, cutting concrete, accessing the pipe and restoring the line, is a separate scope of work. ### Measurable attributes of a slab leak detection service

Slab leak detection carries several measurable attributes that separate a thorough diagnostic from a rough estimate. Location accuracy with tracer gas is approximately plus or minus 6 inches, while acoustic-only methods on older cast-iron lines can widen that margin to 12 inches or more.

Acoustic equipment operates across a defined frequency range measured in Hz. Higher frequencies isolate pinhole leaks in copper supply lines, while lower frequencies carry better through thick concrete and soil. Leak detection sensitivity, expressed as a percentage of minimum detectable flow, determines whether a slow seep registers at all. Professional-grade amplifiers detect leaks that produce no visible surface moisture. Detection range on a single setup extends to several meters along a pipe run, allowing a technician to sweep a full slab section without repositioning equipment repeatedly. Pressure differential readings in psi confirm the severity of the leak. They help prioritize repair timing. A line that loses pressure rapidly needs faster intervention than one holding near-static pressure with a slow seep. ### Common signs that slab leak detection is needed

Slab leak detection is warranted when a building shows unexplained water bill increases, warm or damp spots on the floor, the sound of running water with all fixtures closed, or visible cracks forming in the slab or foundation walls. One indicator stands above the rest. A water meter that continues to move with every supply valve shut is the clearest single sign that a sub-slab line is losing pressure. Mold or mildew odor at floor level, soft spots in flooring, and shifting door frames all point to long-running moisture accumulation beneath the concrete. Signs of an emergency slab leak typically include a sudden pressure drop alongside visible flooding at the base of a wall, or a main line isolation test that shows a complete pressure loss within minutes.

Above-slab plumbing repair and visible leak repair address failures at accessible fixtures, joints and wall-run pipes. They are not substitutes for sub-slab detection when the leak origin is beneath the concrete. Attempting to repair without a confirmed location wastes concrete cuts and risks damaging adjacent lines. ### Where slab leak detection applies

Slab leak detection applies to any structure built on a poured concrete slab, including single-family homes, multi-unit residential buildings, retail spaces and commercial properties. In Edmond and the broader Oklahoma City metro, post-tension slab construction is common, and post-tension cables running through the slab add a constraint. Detection must be precise enough to mark the leak location away from cable paths before any cutting begins. Slab leak detection on commercial properties typically relies on the same acoustic and tracer gas methods used in residential work, but larger floor areas and higher-pressure supply systems can extend detection time and may require multiple equipment setups across a single job.
