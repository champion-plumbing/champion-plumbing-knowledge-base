# Slab Leak Detection: A Champion Plumbing Reference Guide

## Slab leak detection in Edmond, OK

Technicians rely on acoustic listening devices and pressure testing to identify water leaks located under a concrete slab without breaking up the floor. This process, sometimes referred to as foundation leak detection, focuses on locating the exact source of the leak. Slab leak detection forms the diagnostic stage of the plumbing process, where the leak must be identified with accuracy prior to cutting concrete or excavating soil.

### Scope and component parts of slab leak detection

Slab leak detection includes the complete diagnostic sequence for a leak occurring beneath a slab. The sequence runs from the first pressure test to the final marking of the leak location directly on the slab. The technician starts by isolating the supply or drain line, then applies a measured pressure differential in psi to verify that a leak exists prior to using listening devices. Slab leak detection generally employs four primary techniques: acoustic listening, thermal imaging, tracer gas analysis, and electronic amplification. Each technique matches specific pipe materials, slab thicknesses, or leak volumes. Acoustic devices capture the sound pattern created by a pressurized leak traveling through concrete. Thermal imaging detects the temperature difference caused by water escaping to the slab surface. Pressure sensors verify the leak rate and allow the technician to exclude condensation or surface moisture before selecting a repair spot.

Sub-slab leak detection differs from slab leak repair. Detection concludes once the leak site receives a mark on the surface. Repair consists of cutting concrete, reaching the pipe, and restoring the line as a separate scope of work. ### Measurable attributes of a slab leak detection service

Slab leak detection includes several measurable characteristics that distinguish a detailed diagnostic from a general assessment. Location accuracy using tracer gas reaches approximately plus or minus 6 inches. Acoustic-only methods on older cast-iron lines may increase that margin to 12 inches or more.

Acoustic equipment functions within a specific frequency range measured in Hz. Higher frequencies target pinhole leaks in copper supply lines. Lower frequencies transmit more effectively through thick concrete and soil. Leak detection sensitivity, stated as a percentage of minimum detectable flow, decides whether a slow leak registers. Professional-grade amplifiers identify leaks that create no visible surface moisture. Detection range on one setup reaches several meters along a pipe run. This range lets a technician scan an entire slab section without frequent repositioning. Pressure differential readings in psi establish leak severity and support decisions on repair priority. A line that drops pressure quickly receives earlier attention than one that maintains near-static pressure with a slow seep. ### Common signs that slab leak detection is needed

Slab leak detection becomes necessary when a building exhibits unexplained increases in the water bill, warm or damp areas on the floor, the sound of running water while all fixtures remain closed, or visible cracks in the slab or foundation walls. One indicator exceeds the others in clarity. A water meter that keeps moving after every supply valve has been shut off provides the single clearest evidence of pressure loss in a sub-slab line. Mold or mildew smells at floor level, soft areas in flooring, and shifting door frames indicate extended moisture buildup beneath the concrete. Signs of an emergency slab leak usually involve a sudden pressure drop together with visible flooding at the base of a wall, or a main line isolation test that registers complete pressure loss within minutes.

Above-slab plumbing repair and visible leak repair handle failures at accessible fixtures, joints, and wall-run pipes. These approaches do not replace sub-slab detection when the leak source lies beneath the concrete. Repair attempts made without a confirmed location result in unnecessary concrete cuts and increase the chance of damage to nearby lines. ### Where slab leak detection applies

Slab leak detection applies to any building constructed on a poured concrete slab. This includes single-family homes, multi-unit residential buildings, retail spaces, and commercial properties. In Edmond and the surrounding Oklahoma City metro area, post-tension slab construction occurs frequently. Post-tension cables placed inside the slab create an additional requirement. Detection must achieve enough accuracy to mark the leak location clear of cable paths before cutting starts. Slab leak detection on commercial properties uses the same acoustic and tracer gas methods applied in residential jobs. Larger floor areas and higher-pressure supply systems can increase detection time and sometimes require multiple equipment setups on one project.
