## What is slab leak detection?

Slab leak detection, called foundation leak detection, is the specialized process of locating water leaks beneath a concrete building slab without demolishing the floor. It uses acoustic listening equipment, pressure testing, thermal imaging, and tracer gas analysis to pinpoint the exact source of the leak. Precision is the priority. Slab leak detection sits at the diagnostic end of the plumbing trade, finding the leak precisely before any concrete is cut or soil is excavated. ## What is acoustic leak detection?

Acoustic leak detection, called Sonic Leak Detection, is a non-invasive method that locates water leaks by detecting, amplifying, and analyzing the acoustic signals a pressurized pipe produces when water escapes through a crack, joint failure, or corroded section. It is a type of Leak Detection. It sits within the broader category of non-invasive leak detection methods alongside thermal imaging and pressure-based testing. Because it requires no excavation and no wall demolition to find a leak, acoustic leak testing is well suited for buried supply lines, slab-embedded pipes, and finished interior walls where cutting would cause significant damage. ## What is localized pipe repair?

Localized pipe repair, called a targeted pipe fix, addresses damage within a single, defined section of pipe. It does not replace the entire line, reroute the system, or require tearing out walls and flooring across the property. It is a type of pipe repair, sitting under that broader category the way a patch sits under the concept of restoration. The work targets one compromised segment, whether a corroded copper elbow, a cracked PVC joint, a pinhole leak in a galvanized run, or a stress fracture in a cast-iron drain, and restores that segment to full function without disturbing the rest of the system. ## What are grease traps?

Grease traps, called FOG interceptors or grease interceptors, are plumbing devices installed between kitchen drain lines and the municipal sewer system. They intercept fats, oils, and grease before those substances enter the sanitary sewer. A grease trap holds wastewater long enough for FOG to cool, separate from the water, and float to the surface, where baffles hold it in place while cleaner water flows out through the outlet pipe. Commercial kitchens, restaurants, cafeterias, and food-processing facilities in Edmond rely on grease interceptors to stay compliant with local pretreatment ordinances and to keep drain lines clear. ## What is a clogged drain emergency?

A clogged drain emergency, called a Blocked Drain Crisis, is a sudden complete or near-complete blockage that causes immediate water backup and prevents normal drainage flow. It can damage flooring, walls, pipe joints, and surrounding structure if not cleared within hours. This is a type of Plumbing Emergency. It requires the same urgency as a burst pipe or gas leak, not a scheduled maintenance call. A Severe Drain Clog differs from a slow drain in one measurable way: drainage flow rate drops to near zero liters per minute and standing water begins to accumulate rather than recede.

## What is an overflowing toilet emergency?

An overflowing toilet emergency, called a bathroom flooding emergency, is a plumbing failure in which water discharges over the rim of the toilet bowl onto the surrounding floor. The cause is a blockage in the drain line, a malfunction in the fill valve or float assembly, or a failure in the drainage system that prevents water from exiting the bowl at a rate equal to or greater than the rate at which it enters. It is classified as a plumbing emergency for three reasons: the discharge is uncontrolled, damage to flooring, subfloor, and adjacent rooms escalates within minutes, and the water involved may carry sewage contamination that poses a health risk. A toilet overflow is distinct from a slow drain or a running toilet. Those are maintenance issues. An overflowing toilet is an active emergency requiring immediate action.

## What is a no hot water emergency?

A no hot water emergency, called a hot water failure or domestic hot water failure, is a sudden and complete loss of a building's hot water supply. Every fixture draws cold or ambient-temperature water. Causes include a failed water heater, a tripped circuit, a gas supply interruption, a broken thermostat, a failed heating element, a sediment-blocked tank, or a burst supply line. No hot water emergency is a type of Plumbing System Problem. It requires immediate diagnosis because a building without hot water cannot support basic sanitation, cooking, or safe bathing. ## What is a flooded basement?

A flooded basement, called water in basement or basement flooding, is an accumulation of unwanted water in the lowest level of a building. Water intrudes. The consequences extend well beyond the basement itself. Flooded basement is classified as a Building Issue, meaning it affects the structural integrity, air quality, and habitability of the entire property above it. ## What are backflow prevention standards?

Backflow Prevention Standards, called Backflow Codes or Cross-Connection Control Regulations, are a type of plumbing regulation that defines the design, installation, testing and maintenance requirements for devices that stop contaminated water from flowing backward into a clean supply. These standards govern which devices are approved for a given hazard level, how often those devices must be tested, who is qualified to test them and what documentation must be filed with the local water authority. Governing bodies such as the American Society of Sanitary Engineering (ASSE) and the American Water Works Association (AWWA) publish the model codes that most municipalities, including Edmond, adopt and enforce.
